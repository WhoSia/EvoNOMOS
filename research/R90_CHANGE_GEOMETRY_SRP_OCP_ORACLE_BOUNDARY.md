# R90 — Contract-constrained change geometry, SRP separability and OCP option value
Date: 2026-11-24. Mathematical research for Paper B, not an empirical architecture superiority claim.

## Objects and theorem boundaries
Let a finite directed nonnegative weighted graph represent admissible design states and elementary contract-preserving transformations. The admissibility convention must include every intermediate state and edge; a reachable goal may require a different deployment/migration grammar. Extended shortest-path distances satisfy the triangle inequality; asymmetry and infinity are allowed. Zero-cost paths may identify different states. Do not assume a metric. Cartesian product distance addition is established graph theory (Sheridan et al. 2023, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2023.04.019; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2023.02.005). Coordinate projections give a lower bound, and independent lifts of local shortest paths attain it when the graph is precisely the weighted product. Contract x=y removes both intermediate states 01 and 10: under single-coordinate edits 00->11 is unreachable. A cheap joint edit gives 1 instead of product distance 3. A cross-coordinate edge cost penalty gives 8 instead of 3. Therefore dependency need not monotonically increase change cost. Directed product example: 16 pairs, 64 triples, forward 3 reverse 7.

For initial state s and fixed contract T, let F(S) be the least cost of an admissible path to any state satisfying all demands in subset S of a finite ground set U. If adding a demand only restricts the goal set, F is monotone, and F(empty)=0 if s is admissible. In this note restrict to finite real-valued F on the entire Boolean cube; unreachable targets require a separate feasibility flag (infinite differences are undefined). A cost-based decomposition of demand variables does not identify source-file/module boundaries; source semantics and independently observed interventions are needed.

## Lemma 1 — exact separability and finest partition
For a partition P of U define F_P(S)=F(empty)+sum_{B in P}[F(S intersect B)-F(empty)].
For i,j not in S define delta_ij(S)=F(S+i+j)-F(S+i)-F(S+j)+F(S).
Create an undirected graph on U with an edge ij iff some background S has nonzero delta_ij(S).
Then F=F_P for all S iff no edge crosses P. Necessity: each block summand cancels in the cross-block mixed difference. Sufficiency: order S block by block. Write F(S)-F(empty) as telescoping marginals. Moving each marginal from its within-block predecessor set to the full preceding set changes it by a telescoping sum of cross-block mixed differences, all zero. Thus the connected components give the unique finest such partition. This is an elementary finite separability argument, not a claim of a new mathematical theorem; related variable interaction graphs and differential grouping are established (https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/156228/; https://doi.org/10.1145/2725494.2725497; https://doi.org/10.1109/TEVC.2017.2694221).
Counterexample F(S)=|S|+6*1[S={A,B,C}]: all empty-background pair interactions vanish; each pair has a nonzero interaction 6 in the remaining demand's background. All 256 binary-valued functions on three variables were verified by reconstruction. Not all of these functions are monotone path costs; tests target the more general algebraic statement.

## Lemma 2 — approximate separability, sharp bound
Let epsilon_ij=max over backgrounds S of |delta_ij(S)|. Then
|F(S)-F_P(S)| <= sum_{i<j, i,j in S, in different blocks} epsilon_ij.
Proof: use block-ordered marginals as in Lemma 1. Each earlier cross-block variable adds one mixed difference to the marginal of the current variable, so each cross-block pair occurs exactly once. Apply the triangle inequality. Tightness: F(S)=sum over i<j in S of nonnegative pair weights; choose weights 2,3,4 and singleton partition, yielding residual and bound 9 at the full set. Verification: all 256 binary-valued three-variable tables times all five partitions times eight subsets = 10,240 comparisons; one additional tight witness. Mathematical novelty not established. Conditional on a demand distribution P, taking expectations yields E|F-F_P| <= sum epsilon_ij P(i,j both occur); no independence assumption. A proof of this simple inequality is not evidence that epsilon is identifiable or stable in actual software.
Decision corollary: if two or more candidate architectures' expected costs are approximated with uniform error <= eta, a minimizer of the approximate costs has true excess expected cost <= 2 eta among those candidates. Separate feasibility; this is standard approximation reasoning.

## Lemma 3 — exact black-box certification lower bound
For n>=2, deterministic algorithms given arbitrary monotone normalized F and exact point queries cannot certify singleton additivity in the worst case with fewer than 2^n-1 nonempty queries.
Proof: answer with F0(S)=2|S|. If any nonempty T remains unqueried, let F1(S)=F0(S)+1[S=T]. F1 remains normalized and monotone, since every proper one-element inclusion initially has cost gap 2, and perturbation is at most 1. F1 is nonadditive: if |T|>=2, singleton costs are unchanged and the cost at T differs; if |T|=1, the changed singleton and any other singleton predict a pair cost differing from the unchanged pair. The transcript is identical, so a deterministic correct decision is impossible. Both functions can even be realized abstractly by a directed star from the initial state to states labeled by satisfied subsets, with direct edge costs F(S) and a target accepting any superset. This is a black-box model, not a lower bound for actual code analysis; it does not cover bounded-error randomized property testing. Related generic query complexity needs full prior-art audit before any original theorem claim.
Verification: n=2..8, 501 distinct one-point rivals (3+7+15+31+63+127+255), all normalized, monotone and nonadditive; 333,168 cover monotonicity checks (12+84+480+2480+12096+56896+261120).
If a known externally justified interaction order r holds, F(S)=sum_{A subset S, |A|<=r} mu(A). Recover mu(A) by recursive Boolean-lattice Moebius inversion from sum_{k=0}^r binom(n,k) evaluations (one fewer if F(empty)=0 known). n=6,r=2: 22 including empty, reconstruct all 64 states; an additional hidden change at mask 7 evades those evaluations and invalidates the order promise. No low-order guarantee without independent evidence. This conditional result changes research priority from toy enumeration to deriving source-based structural promises, and measuring query cost. 

## OCP as a conditional option, not a universal prescription
Both compared designs must meet the same contracts. With upfront premium h and conditional savings delta_d, positive expected savings require sum p_d delta_d >= h. This is ordinary option-value/decision theory, already close to Baldwin–Clark and Cai–Sullivan. R60 constructed model M=4-2q, S=1+5q: crossing q=3/7. For q in [.3,.6], pure worst regret M=.9, S=1.2; allowing a randomized mixture p of M produces max(.9p,1.2(1-p)), minimized p=4/7, regret 18/35. Checked 1001 grid points. Randomization is mathematical relaxation, not engineering advice to randomize a single codebase. No assumed architecture superiority.
