## 2. Classical option-antitonicity theorem (P14-T1)

If X≼Y then ↑Y⊆↑X and therefore Future(Y)⊆Future(X), for EVERY fixed family Ω. Proof: compose a witness route Y→Z to the actually certified route X→Y, preserving the declared old client, and note that the same final Z witnesses g from X. This is a standard viability/reachability monotonicity theorem, not independent new mathematical priority.

**Important semantic distinction:** Already-satisfied goals may increase as software is extended. This does NOT contradict Future(Y)⊆Future(X), because any goal already satisfied at Y was already *attainable* by some edit path from X. The theorem applies to goal *possibility with future recourse*, not immediate truth or price. No comparison of cost-optima follows automatically from this set inclusion because future edit costs are counted from the new starting point.

For the declared Ω containing customer goals g_E and g_F, at none-world both are feasible (choose E or F later), at E-world only g_E is, and at F-world only g_F is. Strict inclusion holds. This is the **R5 source-backed option-loss mechanism**, recast as an exact order-theoretic statement, not a fresh data discovery.

## 3. Three non-equivalent timing modalities (P14-T2)

Fix a current source support X and a family Ω of goals revealed later. The following are different queries:

* **Adaptive recourse after disclosure:** forall g∈Ω exists Y_g≽X such that g(O(Y_g)); equivalently Ω⊆Future(X). The edit policy may condition on which goal g has been revealed.
* **One irreversible advance commitment before disclosure:** exists Y≽X such that forall g∈Ω, g(O(Y)). The single fixed selected endpoint must satisfy all goals; this is logically stronger. If the real operational contract permits a later additional repair after the advance commitment, the quantifiers must represent that instead, e.g. exists nontrivial initial edit Y≻X such that forall g exists Z_g≽Y with g(O(Z_g)).
* **A nondisclosing adversary with later repair allowed:** exists a policy whose actions at each time depend only on actually observed information; its winning condition depends on the disclosure schedule, delay budget and stagewise old-client contract. This is an observation-based game, not simply either of the first two quantified statements.

**P14-T2 proposition:** For g_E and g_F from R4/R5, at X=(T,none):
 - forall g∈{g_E,g_F} exists finite owned-edit repair Y_g≽X (adaptive feasibility TRUE);
 - no fixed Y can simultaneously satisfy g_E and g_F within this seven-edit grammar, because E and F are mutually source-incompatible (nonadaptive simultaneous-goal feasibility FALSE);
 - every advance Y that contains E rules out future g_F; every advance Y that contains F rules out future g_E. A free advance on Q alone preserves both possibilities, so 'all advance edits are bad' is FALSE. Requiring at least one **E/F-committing** advance is the correct impossibility restriction.

All these conclusions follow from R5's previously source-verified guarded reachability and P13-R3 HTTP observations, rather than from a new Go test. If the system allowed deletions/unmounting or an alternative handler implementing both HTTP contracts, these impossibilities would need reassessment; do not generalize them to arbitrary Go programs.

## 4. Possible query languages, exactness and why this matters for SOLID

An architecture should not be declared future-proof without specifying (a) the family of admissible future client demands Ω, (b) when and what will be observed, (c) which edits the actor owns, (d) actual source effect semantics, (e) stagewise safety and (f) actual or artificial cost budget.

This is a useful *conditional* mathematical foundation for a future concept of OCP/DIP: preserving an extension/abstraction boundary is not 'have many source classes' but retaining admissible **options for a declared client goal family under a legitimate information timeline**. No universal SOLID optimality theorem is asserted. Classical source-aware weighted LTS, safety games and partially observable planning receive equal evidence and may fully solve this instance.
