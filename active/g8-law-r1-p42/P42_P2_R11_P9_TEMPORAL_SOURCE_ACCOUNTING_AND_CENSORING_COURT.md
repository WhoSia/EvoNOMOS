## 4. Non-circular mathematical boundaries

The two executable source snapshots have the same old-client HTTP response and Tee output, but different internal byte-count observations. Hence no function of those selected HTTP observations alone determines the counter. Nested ReadFrom and Write calls must carry event-ownership obligations. If the counting domains of monitors form a disjoint cover of atomic transfers, additive byte accounting is exact; overlap can cause double counting. Proof: partition the finite sum over atomic output events.

At submission time, histories that share an identical prefix can later acquire different review counts and statuses. Therefore exact final review data is not a deterministic function of submission-time information. This is standard filtration reasoning; probabilistic prediction remains possible.

With unknown actual permissions or overlapping repair-cost intervals, the sign of architecture dominance is not identified. Closed-unmerged metadata alone cannot determine feasibility. These are classical causal and transition-system limitations, not a newly established programming-language law.

SOLID's five principles remain operational, conditional subcases of source-port, resource-frame and demand-specific repair criteria. Classical CCR 2.0, Iris, DimSum, Simuliris and costed state-transition systems remain adequate for the observed native examples.
