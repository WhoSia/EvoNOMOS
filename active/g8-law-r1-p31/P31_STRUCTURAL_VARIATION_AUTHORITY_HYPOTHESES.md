## Structural explanatory variables
- Variation-authority placement: independently duplicated rule-conversion knowledge versus shared conversion ownership.
- Cross-client semantic congruence: do ingestion and display need the same behavior under a change request?
- Change co-occurrence: do both client pathways require revision together?
- Boundary tax: abstraction creation, adaptations and obligations imposed on otherwise independent clients.

Two rival hypotheses:
H1: When changes are jointly required and semantics match, shared rule authority may lower independent modification obligations, without necessarily lowering code churn.
H2: When client semantics diverge, a common rule boundary may require extra adapters/branches, reducing or reversing the H1 benefit.

Neither is a discovered law; both have obvious antecedents in information hiding, SRP/ISP, feature scattering and co-change research. A new named variable is not originality. We must compare with simpler coupling/file-count and dependency-fanout baselines and show an incremental held-out prediction.
