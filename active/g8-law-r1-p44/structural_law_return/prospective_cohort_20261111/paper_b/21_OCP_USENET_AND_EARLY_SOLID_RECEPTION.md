## Why the 1995 exchange matters

There are at least three different readings a design reviewer can mistakenly score with the same word. Source-edit necessity asks whether a stated extension can be implemented while preserving a named module's source. Authority to edit asks whether the maintenance actor can edit that module. Caller continuity asks whether existing callers retain a specified behavior. Brown discusses repair and access. Martin answers explicitly in terms of necessity under extension. Torgersen later makes recompilation and pre-existing objects additional dimensions. The [library fixture](./15_MEYER_LIBRARY_CLOSURE.md) separates source preservation from observable caller continuity. Parnas explains why the latter cannot always be read off an invocation edge alone.

This supports a history of recurring ambiguity under changes of genre. In 1995 the mailing-list format preserved an objection and a reply. The 2003 chapter preserved a counterexample to its first shape example. The [2025 review archive](./18_ARE_WE_SOLID_OCP_ARCHIVE_AUDIT.md) preserved a code input and a classification prompt. Individual sources retain different premises. A claim that the initials caused the loss would require evidence of transmission and comparison across more witnesses. No new model run or maintenance effect was measured. LAW-R2 HOLD.
