## Primary comparison

- Meyer 1988, p. 22 and the upper part of p. 23, connects information hiding to continuity for clients: reading private implementation details and being entitled to depend on them are different.
- Meyer 1988, p. 23, §2.3, treats openness as availability for extension and closure as availability for other modules to use through a stable description. For design/specification modules, the text specifies approval, inclusion in the project baseline, and publication of the interface.
- Meyer 1988, pp. 24–25, illustrates adding a technical-report variant to a library system. Existing clients may need recompilation and edits when they discriminate on variants. He presents inheritance as the way to resolve this dilemma. Thus the first edition contains both a rationale in client dependence and a strong claim about the mechanism.
- [Martin 1996](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~downing/papers/OCP-1996.pdf), p. 1, explicitly identifies his formulation as a paraphrase and cites Meyer p. 23. His wording shifts emphasis toward modification, while strategic closure on p. 6 selects kinds of likely change. The [Meyer 1997 second edition](https://bertrandmeyer.com/wp-content/upLoads/OOSC2.pdf), §3.3, retains the stable-interface and baseline definition. Do not present Martin's wording as a literal quotation from Meyer, or frame the original as uniformly cautious.
