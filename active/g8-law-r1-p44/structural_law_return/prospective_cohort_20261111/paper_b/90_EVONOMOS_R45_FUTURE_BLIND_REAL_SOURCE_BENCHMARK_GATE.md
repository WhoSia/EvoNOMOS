## Candidate actual benchmark to execute later

1. **Freeze original source:** choose permitted, versioned historical repositories and preserve exact SHA, original clients and protected behavior. Set a temporal cutoff before prospective maintenance tasks. Never use source or task metadata dated after cutoff to construct features.
2. **Define architectures and feasibility:** direct per-consumer changes, adapter structure, alternative module boundary where functionally comparable. If alternatives do not all meet the same protected contracts, exclude unsafe variants BEFORE evaluating cost; document incompatibility, do not manufacture a low cost.
3. **Freeze pre-change signals:** only issue content, prior change types, interface history, dependency relations and maintainer/tool constraints visible at the cutoff. Do not inspect future holdout edits when deciding boundary. Historical replay is prospective only relative to a sealed cutoff and independently separated data.
4. **Compare strong classical baselines:** Parnas-style likely-to-change information hiding; Baldwin–Clark-style architecture option-value and modularity cost; plus conventional informed heuristic, always-SOLID, always-direct and task-only baselines. Failure to beat the strongest source-grounded classical benchmark prevents a novelty-performance claim.
5. **Evaluate vector and constraints:** compilation, contract correctness, old-client behavior and explicit source permissions are HARD admissibility requirements; time, regressions, review labor, number/dispersion of changes, tool/tokens and future cost are distinct outcomes. Avoid selecting a favorable scalar metric after observing results.
6. **Paired causal comparison:** implement matched held-out tasks against two maintained source forks, counterbalance people/model and order, and predeclare how accumulated fixes and carry-over affect outcome attribution.
7. **Negative controls:** shuffled pre-change signals, known regime reversal, unseen type of change, altered maintainer, long horizon setup-cost crossover and LSP-like behavioral invariance cases. A true deferral action needs a real escalation workflow and its cost.
8. **Principle induction:** derive conditional recommendations from structural intervention outcomes with no prefilled SOLID labels, hold out repositories and future change types, and ask whether the resulting policy predicts beyond classic real-options/information-hiding rules rather than merely renaming them.
