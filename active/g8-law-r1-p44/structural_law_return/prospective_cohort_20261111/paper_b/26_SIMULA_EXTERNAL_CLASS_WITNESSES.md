# Paper B — SIMULA External Classes in the Records

The [National Library of Norway catalogue record](https://api.nb.no/catalog/v1/items/7d94a1dc051ddb5538c01e344f878f0c) and [Hegna's inventory of Kristen Nygaard's private papers, p. 45](https://www.ub.uio.no/fag/informatikk-matematikk/informatikk/faglig/bibliografier/dns/nygaard/KNiRiksarkivet.pdf#page=45) describe the same title as a stencil. The catalogue dates it to 1968, gives six numbered leaves and supplies the series number [S 10]. The inventory's specimen is undated and listed under Z-8 with a cross-reference to the Riksarkivet C series. These records support a lookup in both collections. Their specimens have yet to be compared. For the Common Base first printing, pp. 21 and 54 of the inventory also give a lead at RA-C 8. Different extents in the inventories should be checked against the physical copies.

For Paper B, the mechanism concerns how one program obtains a reusable declaration. Meyer's closure adds a different question about the stable description that a client can rely on. A single declaration can be separately compiled while a replacement that keeps its name changes the behavior of an existing client. Conversely, a repair can change the provider's source yet preserve the client's observed behavior. The latter two possibilities are exhibited in the [existing library reconstruction](./15_MEYER_LIBRARY_CLOSURE.md). This comparison identifies what an account of extension must specify, independent of any transmission claim between authors.

Once the S-10 copy arrives, check the case studies reported, whether external declarations or compilation are a precondition in each, and what previously compiled clients or classes must keep stable. A practical claim about their experimental uses requires those pages.
