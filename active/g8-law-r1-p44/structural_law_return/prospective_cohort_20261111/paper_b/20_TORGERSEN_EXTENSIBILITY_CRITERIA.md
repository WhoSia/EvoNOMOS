## Close reading

- Section 1.1 gives an expression language with literals and addition. Its two extensions are an evaluation operation and a negation expression. Both are specified before comparing representations. Torgersen attributes the name to Wadler (1998) and names Reynolds (1975) and Cook (1990) as earlier discussions.
- Section 2.2 distinguishes source-level extensibility (preserving existing source), code-level extensibility (avoiding recompilation of existing code), and object-level extensibility (keeping preexisting objects usable after extension). The paper reports that all four solutions support code-level extensibility and that the third and fourth support object-level extensibility.
- Sections 2.5–2.7 separately ask whether independently made extensions can be merged, whether creation and client code remain reusable, and how type safety is obtained. The reported Java variants are restricted to linear extension. The fourth solution uses runtime checks in the dispatch framework.
- This is prior art for separating kinds of preservation and the two directions of extensibility. Object survival applies to preexisting data.
