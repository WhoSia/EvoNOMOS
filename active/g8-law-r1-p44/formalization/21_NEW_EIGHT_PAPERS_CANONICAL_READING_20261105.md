## Original paper-by-paper reading

1. [Pnueli & Rosner (1989), On the Synthesis of a Reactive Module, POPL](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1U72kxVpnPOYQMhfIn3E2B7iYHqPtyVcd/view). Their introduction states the **winning strategy against all possible environment scenarios**; for finite input/output domains synthesis is decidable using tree automata, with a doubly exponential complexity statement in the abstract. P44 F10/F11 nonanticipating source repair and alternating quantifiers directly enter already-established reactive synthesis territory. No claim that their specific automata algorithm proves our source rights / patch semantics.

2. [O'Hearn (2020), Incorrectness Logic, PACMPL POPL Article 10](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Y31ZQQzSlzniQ9apZAehMMSZ7v7N0qmh/view). The original distinguishes overapproximating correctness postconditions from **underapproximating reachable results** for incorrectness; error triples represent actual erroneous reachable outcomes. Crucial warning from later discussion: usual separation-logic frame reasoning does not automatically transfer under underapproximation; heap model/semantic conditions matter. P44 should separate SAFE universal overapprox certificates, BAD existential reachable-witness certificates, and UNKNOWN. Neither one successful test nor one abstract error is by itself sufficient for the opposite claim.

3. [Musuvathi, Qadeer & Ball (2007), CHESS: A Systematic Testing Tool for Concurrent Software, MSR TR-2007-149](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1kwxtEVuUu4pC7OHL7U0Q9ppWDc2MF9qd/view). Report stresses reproducible systematic schedule generation, preemption bounding, task scheduling abstraction and fair schedule assumptions. P44 R3 deterministic one-barrier execution is merely one witness; proving absence of temporal contract violation requires a declared coverage bound and model. CHESS is a **technical report** and was filed 11_SOURCES, not miscategorized as an ordinary conference journal article.

4. [Jimenez et al. (2024), SWE-bench, ICLR](https://drive.google.com/file/d/122kO3_e8zut6sqKCOKMdU4kegrXPuMai/view). Original benchmark contains 2,294 issue/PR tasks from 12 Python repositories and uses executable test cases, pre/post changes and evaluation contexts; its discussion explicitly warns that tests alone do not establish all correctness. Historical Python tasks do NOT by themselves validate a Go-source-rights soundness theorem. A future EvoNOMOS maintainer cohort should freeze before-source, issue text, tests and later patch separately, with no future-fix leakage.

5. [Ouyang, Yang & Zhang (2024), Benchmarking Automated Program Repair, ISSTA](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1iAvBfMP4bB3swzyyUCt52lvd_j3GoRTM/view). The original analyzes 1,814,652 generated patches over a controlled collection of real and mutation-based bugs, including 1,700 synthetic MuBench bugs. Distinguishes compilability, plausible vs genuine patches, syntactic equivalence (SYE), trivial compiler equivalence (TCE). Threats-to-validity note generated mutant bugs may poorly represent real bugs. For P44, use strong equal-data baselines and do not conflate source edit candidate count with distinct semantic outcomes or measured developer effort.

6. [Petke, Martinez, Kechagia, Aleti & Sarro (2024), Patch Overfitting Problem, FSE Companion](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1yDd18DJJDqCY_9JEjzRIUKKIuBwxDf_j/view). Analyzed ten APR tools on studied Defects4J bugs, reporting a median of two plausible patches per bug across their studied tool setting; proposes RANDOM SELECTION as an evaluation baseline for patch assessment. This conflicts with a naive assumption that automatically generated repairs always create huge reviewer workload; P44 source-certificate value must be tested against a trivial baseline, not just no verification.

7. [Sjøberg et al. (2005), Controlled Experiments Survey, IEEE TSE](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1YZkEcpqQlT4uxGZs4z97R2T9MaV4JKa5/view). Found 103 controlled-experiment articles out of 5,453 examined publications during 1993–2002, reports subjects, team/professional/student differences, task/environment scope, internal/external validity and replication. Directly limits claims that synthetic edit units quantify real developer maintainability.

8. [Hannemann & Kiczales (2002), Design Pattern Implementation in Java and AspectJ, OOPSLA](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1VguYU18udHTO_57jrvq5kGb_IGYvASVB/view). Their original comparison reports 17/23 pattern implementations showing modularity improvements under AspectJ on code locality/reusability/composition/pluggability, especially crosscutting roles, while noting limitations e.g. Decorator dynamic ordering, Proxy distinct object needs. Neither the pattern label nor a universal claim of greater maintainability follows; source implementation, legal effects and target demand remain essential.

## Mathematical synthesis, explicitly ours and explicitly classical

O'Hearn shows one *realizable* bad trace can certify a violation; a universal safety proof requires a sound overapproximation of all relevant traces, not just absence of observed failures. This motivates a conditional 3-way P44 repair-checker verdict PROVED_SAFE / PROVED_VIOLATION / UNKNOWN per protected release checkpoint. One certified bad intermediate releases makes a given path unsafe, while a success witness on only one execution cannot prove it safe. The checker must include separate evidence of underapprox witness provenance and overapprox source semantic coverage. Incorrectness logic and ordinary abstract interpretation already contain the principle; NOT a newly discovered law.

Other sources imply a strong research-design change: (i) adversarial future demand strategy needs actual source rights and no forecast leakage; (ii) schedule proof needs coverage beyond one barrier; (iii) benchmark against real and artificial bugs with genuinely strong random/cheap baselines and semantic patch duplication awareness; (iv) engineer time must be separately measured with valid experimental design.
