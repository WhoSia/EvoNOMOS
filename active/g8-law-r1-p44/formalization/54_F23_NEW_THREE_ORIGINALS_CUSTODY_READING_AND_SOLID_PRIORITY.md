## Hennessy–Milner: full 25-page PDF text checked, key theorem/argument
- The program meaning is defined via how a process communicates with an observer. Observational congruence requires indistinguishability in *every supported program context*; it is not simply equality of two chosen HTTP response samples.
- Their progression through finite behavior languages provides algebraic axiomatizations; Section 3 Theorem 3.1 characterizes the no-internal-step observation congruence using associative, commutative, idempotent nondeterministic choice and nil identity. Later sections treat silent actions, concurrency, and richer contexts where naive algebraic laws change. Finite complete axiomatisation requires the declared language/signature; no automatic theorem for Go source or revision/authorization effects.
- EvoNOMOS implication: old-client contextual equivalence is a classical semantic base. Appending a rights-labelled future-edit relation to a static contextual semantics changes the *question*, but does not establish mathematical priority or an empirical predictive improvement. A candidate future-repair congruence must match legitimate edit operations and their costs in addition to client observations.

## Meyer: verified SECOND EDITION; detailed critical reading of preface and Chapter 3, contracts/inheritance navigation
- Preface identifies a structuring method, reliability discipline (contracts), epistemic principle (abstract data types) and classification (inheritance). It is an error to present his book as simply endorsing Robert Martin's later five SOLID labels.
- Chapter 3 separates five **modularity criteria** and resulting five rules and five principles, NOT the same set as SOLID. Its rules include few/small/explicit interfaces and information hiding, while its principles include uniform access, open-closed and single choice.
- Section 3.3 states OCP as the apparent tension of a module being both **open for extension** and **closed for use with stable approved interface**. This is richer than the slogan `never modify existing source` and explicitly recognizes developer/manager conflict. Source-stable extension has value under named consumers and owners, not for all future demands.
- The book's design-by-contract apparatus supports old-client obligations and behavioral substitution, but contract equivalence alone cannot represent all code-edit rights or time-indexed source history. The complete 1,406-page book has **NOT** been read cover-to-cover in this run; detailed reading targeted introduction, modularity chapter and selected relevant contract/inheritance passages. Keep that reading boundary explicit.
