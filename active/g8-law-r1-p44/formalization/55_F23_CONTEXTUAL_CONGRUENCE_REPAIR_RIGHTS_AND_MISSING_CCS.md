# P44-F23 R2 — Contextual Equivalence Is Not a Future Edit-Right Congruence
Date 2026-11-05; theory priority and counterexample note; NOT a new-law claim.

## The three primary source readings that matter
- Hennessy & Milner (1985) original JACM, §2 and §3–§5: an observer experiment and full-context congruence are standard semantic instruments. The authors give equational completeness theorems for specified finite process-language fragments; a process being congruent to another within that fragment does not quantify over editor permissions or future source patches.
- Meyer (1997) original 2nd ed., Ch.3 §§3.1–3.3: **five modularity criteria, five rules, five principles are NOT the later SRP/OCP/LSP/ISP/DIP acronym**. His particular open/closed principle reconciles clients' need for a stable reusable interface with the developer's need for future extension. It is not an axiom banning every change of old source. The preface also explicitly names contract-based reliability, abstract data types and inheritance. Substituting the five SOLID slogans for Meyer's own conceptual architecture misreads this primary source.
- Borodin & El-Yaniv (1998), original image-only scanned Cambridge book: competitive analysis explicitly distinguishes an online history-dependent actor from an offline policy with full future information; Ch.1 introductory list-update theorem, later chapter table of contents on randomization and metrical task systems visually checked. Do not claim machine-searchable full text. No online disadvantage follows if every future edit can wait until after disclosure, or if reversible choices eliminate forced commitment.

## Formal object and a useful but classical obstruction
For a fixed protected client context class C, let `p ~ctx q` mean the source programs are behaviorally equivalent under every admissible client evaluation context in C. In contrast, define an authorized edit graph `G_pi` with source history, actor-specific legal revisions, cost labels, and protected intermediate observations. An edit-right repair congruence R requires (i) preservation of joint protected observations and (ii) matchability of each legal edit and its cost/rights label (under strong bidirectional assumptions), hence preserves future path cost sets as P42 RC1 already proved.

Countermodel: Two runtime programs have the same source behavior under all C (even identical binary/runtime output), but policy π_A grants app actor permission to add a necessary extension while policy π_B forbids that edit. For demand d, complete declared graph A has one safe extension edit of cost 1; complete graph B has no legal edit. Thus `p ~ctx q` while `K_A(p,d)=1` and `K_B(q,d)=∞`. The difference is in the ***system including its authorization state***, not in Hennessy–Milner program semantics. The argument is elementary and anticipated by P42's original RC1/right-sensitive quotient. Do NOT present as original discovery.

Further two-stage residual obstruction was tested in F22: identical individual-demand cost profiles do not guarantee identical staged repair sequence costs. Hence a candidate *minimal* repair-relevant abstraction needs a certified residual language or sufficient labelled simulation; a scalar modularity index, source LOC, number of interfaces, or five SOLID flags cannot generally recover it.

## Candidate source-certification theorem, and precisely where novelty would be
Assume an extractor E from actual versioned source, declared component ownership and permits, client observational contracts and artifact/build context into a finite weighted transition/alternating game abstraction. To prove a sound *winning* certificate, the extractor must show every required client/environment action is covered and every abstract editor action has an implementable legally authorized source edit with protected checkpoint traces; on cost claims retain a lower-bound simulation and constructive source upper witnesses. Under a valid simulation, abstract wins can be transported to source. **This is standard simulation/game reasoning**, and the challenging problem is practical, nontrivial and reliable extraction plus certificate reuse at lower all-in cost than equally informed classical approaches. A counterexample obtained by removing any rights, alias, source version or observer coordinate is a *validity test*, not necessarily a new mathematical theorem.
