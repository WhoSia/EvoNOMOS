# Critical-review gate: why R2 success would still not justify LAW-R2

A fully successful original-source R2 would raise evidence for the named independent route tasks and protect the initial 12 named requests. It would not demonstrate a universally more evolvable structure.

The tasks are researcher-chosen, simple disjoint GET route additions, and are not randomly sampled from maintained customer demand. Classical shortest paths in the supplied rights grammar predict the entire result. Normalization cost is fixed by the earlier selected contract, and the two systems do not have a common full HTTP contextual semantics proof.

Stop or change direction if future R2 experiments merely replicate predicted ties under fresh route names. Candidate R3 should select independent real maintenance changes, instrument coupling and alias/middleware effects, and use a genuine source-aware semantic baseline with equal evidence and compute.

Never collapse source edit count into engineer labor, never silently broaden edit rights, and never relabel exploratory order permutations as externally blinded requirements.
