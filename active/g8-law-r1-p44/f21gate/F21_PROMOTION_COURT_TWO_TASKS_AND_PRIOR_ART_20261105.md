## R1 — A repaired empirical design distinction, not a new program law
Two different tasks were improperly bundled under one requirement of hiding issue titles until the prediction:

(A) Demand-blind forecast: at t0 source S and model M are fixed; specific demand d does not yet exist or is not disclosed; at t0 a forecast of demand-specific outcome is sealed; then demand d arrives. Genuine blindness is needed.

(B) Demand-conditional, outcome-blind forecast: at t0 (<t1) model M and source S are fixed; at t1 independent demand d becomes public and is legitimate input to the frozen predictor f_M(S,d); at t1+ its repair-feasibility or edit-family prediction is sealed; at t2 independent repair outcome/evidence is revealed. **No requirement to hide d at t1+**; it is essential to prohibit leaked future patches, fix evidence, or model tuning after t1. The distinction makes external issue prediction operationally testable without the unnecessarily stronger demand-blind task, provided the outcome is not already available.

A GitHub public issue can have creation time == disclosure time. The old classifier rejected this normal boundary. Another old bug classified a future-created issue with inconsistent live flag as a C4 backtest. The two counterexamples were first reproduced as failing tests, then fixed. The corrected old classifier stays a legacy/provenance gate; new typed `EvaluateForecast` explicitly distinguishes A and B. Neither struct automatically authenticates a seal.

Under both tasks, observations are relative to named old-client contexts, authorized source revisions, a complete or explicitly bounded repair grammar, and fixed budgets. A successful repair *witness* establishes possibility only, not optimality or general old-client equivalence. A bounded failed search cannot certify impossibility.
