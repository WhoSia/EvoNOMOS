## P1.1 — Exact frontier: predictions can differ only because operational rivals are restricted

Let \(\mathcal F(I,B)\) denote predictors computable from exactly the same available evidence \(I\) within a rigorously specified *total* budget \(B\). Let \(L(f,Y)\) be a prechosen predictive loss and \(\mathcal D\) the prospective population of cases. If \(H\in\mathcal F(I,B)\), then

\[
\inf_{f\in\mathcal F(I,B)}\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}L(f,Y)
\leq\mathbb E_{\mathcal D}L(H,Y).
\]

The inequality is simply set inclusion. It **does not** guarantee that a **specific implementable** baseline algorithm \(B_{\mathrm{impl}}\) reaches the infimum. To claim prospective practical superiority we must freeze such a strong real implemented comparator, the same evidence and budget, and an actual prediction difference **before** the holdout outcome is observed. This is a bounded-method comparative question; winning does not logically overturn complete classical μ-calculus reachability or the wider prior-art hypothesis class.

If a proposed EvoNOMOS feature is a deterministic function \(T(I)\) of shared evidence \(I\), letting H consume a free \(T(I)\) without charging its construction gives H a larger effective input/budget. The *only fair options* are (a) charge H to construct \(T\), or (b) give B the same cached \(T\) with allocation cost disclosed. Owner-/obligation-indexed abstraction is **not** epistemically extra if computable from data both can read, but might accelerate some resource-restricted algorithms.

## P1.3 — Preprocessing and demand-horizon sign reversal (CLASSICAL countermodel)

Let H incur a one-time, charged source-derived owner-index construction cost \(F\), followed by \(h\) work units per future demand; let B use no index and cost \(b\) units per demand. Across \(n\) independent future demands over the **same frozen source**:

\[
C_H(n)=F+nh,\qquad C_B(n)=nb.
\]

For \(F=12,h=1,b=3\), the difference \(C_H(n)-C_B(n)=12-2n\). At \(n<6\), the no-index competitor is strictly cheaper; at \(n=6\), both tie; at \(n>6\), H wins. This is a **conditional design-choice reversal** that can be mathematically genuine yet entirely classical amortized analysis. A strong B may choose to create exactly the same index, eliminating even this difference; therefore the example is **not a strong-rival separator or an EvoNOMOS empirical software law**. The demand count, index stability, invalidation cost, maintenance policy and computation units MUST be measured and preregistered in any real trial. A code change invalidating the index increases F or rebuild frequency, shifting the crossing.

A useful falsifier for future real-world P38: if the number of independently specified future demands and total paid preprocessing/work costs fail to predict the sign of the index/no-index reversal within preregistered resource tolerance, the simple amortized candidate is defeated. But even if confirmed, priority lies with classical amortized analysis until a **nonredundant conditional structure** and stronger rival disagreement appear.
